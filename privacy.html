MiniMail

Privacy policy

Last updated: November 26, 2026

MiniMail is a free, open source desktop email client for macOS. It runs on your computer and connects directly to your email provider. MiniMail has no server of its own: its developer does not receive, store or read your mail, your passwords or your Google user data. The source code is public.

Google user data MiniMail accesses

When you choose to connect a Google account, MiniMail asks for:

  • Your name and email address, to add the account and label it.
  • Gmail access (https://mail.google.com/), the permission Google requires for IMAP and SMTP. It covers your messages, their headers, attachments and folders.

How it is used

Google user data is used only to provide the mail features you see in the app:

  • listing, searching, opening and downloading your mail and attachments;
  • writing, saving and sending messages;
  • marking, moving, archiving, marking as spam and deleting messages, including deleting forever when you ask;
  • sorting mail into categories, notifications, unsubscribing and the optional features described below.

MiniMail does not use your data for advertising, does not sell it and does not build profiles from it.

Where it is stored

  • Everything is stored on your computer, in MiniMail's application data folder.
  • Sign-in tokens, app passwords, AI keys, the mail list and the text of downloaded messages are encrypted with a key kept in the macOS Keychain.
  • Connections to Google use TLS.

What leaves your computer

Your mail goes between your computer and your email provider. The exceptions are the features below.

  • AI sorting (off until you add your own AI provider key). For each new conversation, the sender, the subject and about 500 characters of the text are sent to the provider you chose: the Vercel AI Gateway, OpenRouter or TypeSafe. Runs of digits and the path and query of links are replaced with placeholders first.
  • Reading verification codes (needs your AI key). The sender, the subject and the start of an email that holds a code may be sent to the chat model you chose, with links masked.
  • Text completion (off until you turn it on). While you write, your draft, the subject, the recipients, the message you are answering and the few before it are sent to the provider you chose.
  • Command line for AI agents (off until you turn it on). Programs you run on your computer can read and organize your mail within the permissions you set. What such a program does with what it reads is governed by that program and its provider.
  • Sender logos and photos. Company logos are looked up from the sender's domain. People's photos (off by default) send a hash of the sender's address to Gravatar.
  • Account setup may send your email address's domain to Mozilla's mail settings service.
  • Unsubscribing contacts the address the mailing list gave, only when you ask.
  • Updates are checked and downloaded from GitHub. No mail data is sent.

The providers you choose handle what they receive under their own terms. MiniMail does not use Google user data to create, train or improve any generalized artificial intelligence or machine learning model, and does not send it to anyone for that purpose.

Limited Use

MiniMail's use of information received from Google APIs, and its transfer to any other app, adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The use of information received from Google Workspace scopes adheres to the Google User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Removing an account in MiniMail deletes its sign-in tokens, its saved mail list and its saved message text from your computer. It does not delete mail from your Google account.
  • To delete everything MiniMail keeps, quit it and delete the folder ~/Library/Application Support/MiniMail.
  • To withdraw MiniMail's access to your Google account, remove it in your Google Account's connections.

This website

minimail.tech uses Vercel Web Analytics, which counts visits without cookies. The website never has access to your mail or your Google account.
